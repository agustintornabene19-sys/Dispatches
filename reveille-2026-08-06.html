Fighting from the bottom of a paper cup.

"At dawn on 3 October 2009, Camp Keating, a vulnerably exposed US Army outpost in Afghanistan's Nuristan Province, within the Hindu Kush mountain range, came under sustained attack from Taliban fighters. The brutal, close-contact fighting would last all day. It was, recalled one who was there, like 'fighting from the bottom of a paper cup'. By its end, what became known as the Battle of Kamdesh saw eight of the defenders dead, 27 wounded and up to 200 Taliban killed. Two men, Staff Sergeants Clinton Romesha and Ty Carter, were awarded the Medal of Honor, America's highest award for valour."

Syria's Break With Russia Must Go Beyond Oil

"Syria's current leaders and Russia once stood on opposite sides of the battlefield. Yet since the overthrow of former Syrian President Bashar al-Assad, Moscow's longtime ally during the Syrian civil war, the government of President Ahmad al-Sharaa has relied heavily on Russia to meet Syria's critical energy needs. In May, Russian oil deliveries to Syria reportedly reached roughly 60,000 barrels a day, all transported aboard U.S.-sanctioned vessels.

Now Damascus appears prepared to loosen that dependence. Reuters reported that the Syrian government agreed to 'drastically reduce imports of Russian oil' after Washington made the issue part of negotiations for removing Syria from the U.S. list of state sponsors of terrorism — the last major American sanctions regime still imposed on the country.

Pulling Syria out of Russia's sphere of influence would be an important step in aligning it more closely with American goals... But reducing that dependence will not, by itself, remove Russia's influence over the country. Russia still retains access to its two principal military facilities on Syria's coast, the naval facility at Tartus and Hmeimim Air Base in Latakia."

— Ahmad Sharawi, Foundation for Defense of Democracies · Read ›

The Axis of Aggressors vs. America

"North Korean troops and munitions beef up Russia's war against Ukraine. In return, Russia provides North Korea with sophisticated technology for its missiles, drones, satellites, and submarines. Tehran has sent Moscow drones. Repaying that favor, Moscow reportedly provides intelligence to help the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps target American bases in the Middle East. Beijing is believed to give Moscow microelectronics for ballistic, cruise, and hypersonic missiles. There are credible reports that Beijing now plans to send Tehran hundreds of portable shoulder-launched surface-to-air missiles capable of shooting down American aircraft. Discounted Iranian oil keeps Chinese refineries running. Do you see a pattern?

Several years ago, Prof. Waller Newell — perhaps the world's leading scholar on tyrants throughout history — observed the increasing alignment among America's four principal adversaries... arguing that four of the world's most tyrannical rulers appeared to have formed an 'axis' with the goal of establishing a new international order — one in which they, not the U.S., would call the shots."

Enhanced Oil Recovery Aims to Greatly Expand the U.S. Oil Supply

"Enhanced oil recovery (EOR) is a suite of techniques for maximizing production from oil fields after conventional extraction techniques have been exhausted... EOR, in contrast, aims to change the chemical properties of the remaining oil by injecting one of a number of possible gases into the well, lowering the viscosity of the remaining oil and increasing pressure in the well.

If enhanced oil recovery, in particular carbon dioxide-based techniques, could be applied at scale to the United States' massive unconventional shale oil resources, the effect could be the equivalent of growing the current U.S. oil supply by around 50% or possibly even more, likely extending U.S. energy independence and exports for several more decades and reducing global reliance on oil from the Middle East. A handful of U.S. companies are on the forefront of developing enhanced oil recovery technology, most notably Occidental Petroleum, also commonly known as Oxy."
