The Mizrahis will own Israel [more · less · never again]

UnHerd · Ben Judah

In the Forties, as Israel came into being, the word Mizrahi — or "Easterner" — settled in the still-reviving Hebrew language as a catch-all term for Jews flooding in from across the Muslim world. For a society pioneered and founded by Ashkenazi Jews from Europe, the term encompassed everyone from destitute Iraqi Jews, expelled by the plane-load from Baghdad, to illiterate Yemenite Jews airlifted out of medieval poverty, to boat-load after boat-load of Moroccans. Theirs was not a happy arrival. The promised land, the ache of millennia , turned out for most to be shanty-filled refugee camps at best, or a bus to an isolated colony or abandoned Arab village, with some of the previous inhabitants now raiding across the border.

From the Branch

Small Wars Journal on defining unconventional warfare

“Determining what is or is not unconventional warfare (UW) is rarely simple because the answer depends on the level of analysis. At the practitioner level, the question is how it looks: does it resemble the French Resistance maquis in World War II, the stay-behind operations in the Philippines, or Robin Sage, the Special Forces qualification exercise? At the policy level, does it fit current legal authorities and the organization's funding and approval lines? At the doctrinal level, do the exact words match the latest publication or the catchphrase of the moment? Each of these searches for what makes UW distinct from other forms of warfare, and each looks in a different place. Most arguments over what is or is not UW are made at the wrong level, leading to logical fallacies. The way past them is to go deeper, to the concept the verdict is supposed to describe.”
