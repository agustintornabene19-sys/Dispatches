The Right Argues With Itself
Talafreakout [more · less · never again]
Raw Egg Nationalist · ~10 min
Since the Second World War, the left has done its very best to pathologise being right wing: not just explicit identification with parties of the right, but also the basic attitudes, beliefs and stances associated with being of a conservative temperament. The goal of that endeavour is to discourage and even punish right-wing affiliation, to the social and electoral benefit of the left. Theodor Adorno, of the Frankfurt School, landed some heavy early blows with his 1950 book, The Authoritarian Personality. Rooted in Freudian ideas of repression and the Oedipus complex, the book argued for the existence of a specific personality structure that predisposes people to undemocratic and fascist beliefs... Critics pointed out that The Authoritarian Personality had nothing to say about the traits shared by, say, Stalinists. Didn't they also worship power, persecute dissent, believe hysterical conspiracies about their enemies and lapse into terrifying pogroms and bouts of mass-murder? The historian Christopher Lasch, author of The Culture of Narcissism and The Revolt of the Elites, said the book effectively made leftism the sole bastion of true mental health, and risked defining large swathes of American society as a kind of open-air "insane asylum." But these criticisms are moot, really: Adorno wanted to obfuscate leftist terror and he hated the average American and his entire way of life.
Lose, and Lose, and Lose, and Win [more · less · never again]
Palladium Magazine · ~12 min
On Brazil's Election Day I was talking to Renan Santos, the Mission Party candidate for President. He had recently cast his vote in front of all the news cameras... As Renan was telling us about the Mission Party's history, he said that, to run an outsider political campaign, you have to be willing to "lose, and lose, and lose, and win." After twelve years of campaigning, Renan and the Free Brazil Movement had hoped that this election would be the beginning of victory, with an impressive showing in the presidential race and serious gains in the legislature. It was not to be, as we learned that night. Renan came in fourth place with 2.6 million votes, 2.2% of the total, and his party kept its single seat in the federal legislature but failed to add any more. It was a thoroughly mediocre result. The Mission Party boys are dusting themselves off and plugging along, continuing to build up the movement's fundamentals—cadre, doctrine, mass mobilization—and get ready for next time.

Yemen's Widening War
Sending Syrian Soldiers to Yemen Would Be a Mistake
Ahmad Sharawi, FDD / Foreign Policy · ~7 min
In June, U.S. President Donald Trump floated an extraordinary proposal to deal with Hezbollah: Syria should enter Lebanon and finish off the group. Few leaders had more reason to oblige than Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa. Hezbollah had spent years fighting alongside Bashar al-Assad's forces. Even after Assad's fall, the group continued to sow instability across Syria's western border. Damascus had every reason to settle the score, and Sharaa still refused. He insisted that his government sought cooperation with Syria's neighbor, not another war. Four months later, Sharaa appears to be contemplating a foreign intervention, this time more than a thousand miles from home. Following Sharaa's Oct. 6 meeting with Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman in Riyadh, Axios and Reuters reported that the Saudis were seeking Syrian military assistance against the Houthis in Yemen, where Saudi-backed forces have been battling the group since early September.

L'OIT dans la tourmente
Le Monde diplomatique · ~8 min
La possibilité de déplacer la production là où les salaires sont plus bas et les protections sociales plus faibles met les salariés du monde entier en concurrence. Depuis sa création en 1919, par le traité de Versailles aux côtés de la Société des nations (SDN), l'Organisation internationale du travail (OIT) élabore des normes pour empêcher que les conditions imposées aux uns servent à dégrader celles des autres. Elle a inventé le tripartisme, c'est-à-dire le dialogue entre les gouvernements et les représentants de ceux qui travaillent et qui les emploient. Aujourd'hui encore, il s'agit de la seule institution mondiale réunissant représentants des gouvernements et des partenaires sociaux de 187 pays... La mondialisation libérale a cependant renforcé la logique du moins-disant social : aux États de rendre leur main-d'œuvre "attractive," aux salariés de justifier le coût de leurs protections. Le sortant togolais Gilbert Houngbo peut faire valoir certaines avancées. Sous son mandat, l'OIT a adopté des conventions sur les dangers biologiques et le travail des plateformes. La saisine de la Cour internationale de justice à son initiative a également permis de confirmer la protection du droit de grève, contestée par les représentants patronaux.

Aesthetics & the Good Life
The Prayer of Kings, It's All Different Now, Rules of the Dining Room [more · less · never again]
The Pillar · ~9 min
Earlier this week was the feast of St. Edward, King of England, known as the Confessor, as opposed to his uncle, also king, also named Edward, but known as the Martyr. For reasons I doubt need explaining, I have a particular devotion to St. Edward... When I worked in Westminster, I used to walk across the square to pray at his tomb every year. It was a small silly pleasure of mine to enjoy barging past the queues of tourists and, when challenged by the berobed ticket collector, announce loudly that I was a Christian there to pray at the tomb of the saint and isn't simony a sin, even in the Church of England? All these churches cannot, in good conscience or policy, charge members of the faithful to come in and pray. And if you announce that as your intention, they will let you in. But they will assign some beadle or warder or verger or prebendery or whatever to shadow you and make sure you don't look at any of the celebrity tombs or take a picture of the ceiling or anything. It always felt like a still, small act of pious defiance kneeling to pray at St. Edward's tomb on his feast day, with some officious human spud in a gown lurking over my shoulder incredulously. On one occasion, there was a positive cluck of exasperation when I took out a rosary.
