Yemen, the Red Sea, and the Widening War
Can anyone stop the Houthis?
Reuel Marc Gerecht, FDD / UnHerd · ~9 min
"Yemen has always been defined by its topography. A landscape of narrow coastal plains, mountainous highlands, and a desert interior, has helped preserve tribal identities, blurring and softening sharp religious divisions... Combining a deep knowledge of their country's landscape with revolutionary zeal, the Houthis have coalesced into an unrivaled fighting force, as well as Iran's best allies in its war against America and Israel – costing Tehran surprisingly little money. Indeed, the more you learn about the Houthis, the less surprising it becomes that the group should have embarrassed the US Navy in 2024 and 2025... Only the Israelis, who briefly got into a missile exchange with the Houthis in 2025, have managed to deter them: they went after the Houthis' oil supplies, energy infrastructure, ports, airports, and, perhaps most important, senior personnel. Sure enough, the Houthis stopped firing at Israel. But either way, it's little wonder that the Houthis are now back, seizing land along the Red Sea littoral and the islands of Perim and Greater and Lesser Hanish."

Aesthetics & the Good Life
Don't Count Out the Country House [more · less · never again]
Will Tanner, Palladium Magazine · ~8 min
"The wealthy of Britain, and those of the Western world more broadly, are today expected to surrender rather than pass on the fortune they have spent their lives creating. The 'haves' are tolerated, and at times even encouraged, to spend exorbitantly on pleasure and frivolity, so long as upon death they hand whatever remains over to bureaucrats putatively aiding the have-nots... What is less known, or if known, rarely discussed, is that this attitude is the exact antithesis of what made Western civilization great in the first place. In almost every era other than our own, the role of wealth was understood to be for building an enduring dynasty to carry on the values and legacy of those who had contributed so much to society. Before the Great War, British society expected that those born into wealth, whether as a result of a dynast landing at Pevensey Bay with William the Conqueror or an uncle industrializing Manchester, would spend their privileged lives in roles of leadership and public service, before passing on their carefully stewarded riches for their heirs to do the same."
