The Long Reads
We Cannot Understand the Greeks
Imperium Press

"Gildhelm is right: unlike for our great-great-grandfathers, the classical Greeks leave us cold. And he is right that this is owing to a civilizational catastrophe. But the time of the Greek renaissance is coming, though it will not be the same Greeks as before who are celebrated. The 19th century did love the Greeks, but it took Nietzsche to ask it, 'which Greeks?' Behind his question lies a subtle prescription: look to the terrifying Bronze Age Greeks as the model rather than the later, decadent classical Greeks of Socrates, Euripides, and Pericles.

We, as a civilization, have no affinity for the classical Greeks anymore. We cannot 'feel' them, cannot grasp them as models—they feel foreign to us in a way they did not to a Victorian schoolboy. And that is a good thing, because they can offer us no help in our present situation. They could not even help themselves. Two-and-a-half centuries after the trial of Socrates, Greece was conquered forever by the stronger, more archaic Rome. That is about the duration from the Enlightenment to now.

...These Greeks were men for whom the other was not only not Greek, but scarcely a man—grist for his mill, at best a resource capable of being instrumentalized. They were men who understood that authority comes from without, that the world is made of will."
