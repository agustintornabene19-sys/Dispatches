Dragoons, and the long arms they carried.
"Dragoons throughout the world shared one obvious commonality. However tall they might be, and whatever the size of the horses they rode, they all carried long arms of some sort... When visiting Prussian garrisons, McClellan learned that, of the hundred and thirty dragoons of each squadron who carried shoulder arms... fifty carried rifled carbines and eighty abbreviated muskets of the smooth-bore persuasion. The custom of giving a shoulder-arm of some sort to each dragoon who rode in the ranks owed much to memories of the seventeenth century, when soldiers armed with long barreled pieces called 'dragons' rode to the places where they planned to fight on foot."
