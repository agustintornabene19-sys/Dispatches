Why Europe Isn't Ready for NATO 3.0
"The Trump administration's anticipated reduction of US forces from Europe hit a reality check last week. Two overlapping missions, one by CIA Director John Ratcliffe to Moscow and one by Under Secretary of Defense for Policy Elbridge Colby to Stuttgart and Brussels, inadvertently exposed potential flaws in the Pentagon's ongoing retrenchment strategy. While Ratcliffe was in Moscow reportedly to dissuade an expected Russian attack on NATO, Colby was preparing allies for their new role: assuming primary responsibility for deterring the Kremlin with conventional combat power. The timing and purpose of both missions highlight major questions that the transition to 'NATO 3.0' has not yet answered.

On paper, NATO 3.0 is meant to strengthen Europe's armed forces after decades of neglect. Announced in February 2026, it closely follows the Pentagon's National Defense Strategy (NDS)."

Ankara Is Baiting Israel, Not the Reverse
"Before dawn on August 18, Israeli jets destroyed the runway at Syria's Abu al-Duhur air base in Idlib. Within hours, America's ambassador in Ankara, Tom Barrack, pronounced the raid an unnecessary escalation. Days later he floated the theory that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu was baiting the Turks for his political advantage ahead of Israel's October election. The entire episode reveals how badly Washington has mismanaged the rise of Turkey amid profound changes taking place across the Middle East.

Israel struck because it had intelligence, that it shared with Washington six days beforehand, implicating Turkey's intention to place radar, air defenses, and troops at the base. In the wake of calls by the Turks to raise a pan-Islamic army to attack Israel, not to mention Ankara's support for Hamas, the Israelis were not inclined to look the other way. This is the 'October 8 mentality' touted by the Israeli defense establishment. No longer will the Jewish state allow for threats to gather on its doorstep."
