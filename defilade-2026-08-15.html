Tehran's Reckoning
Checkmate In Tehran: Converting Trump's Economic Pressure Into Victory [more · less · never again]
Janatan Sayeh · FDD · ~7 min

"President Donald Trump said that his policy toward Iran has shifted to 'low-keying it' and 'semi-negotiating,' while noting the regime struggles to pay its troops. In essence, Washington is now being reactive rather than proactive, letting pressure shape the next opportunity. And rightly so, as widening Tehran's internal fractures offers more leverage than strikes that cost more to carry out than the targets do to replace. But the question is whether Washington will be prepared to act when the moment arrives. Economic pressure has repeatedly acted as a catalyst for nationwide movements in Iran and can also erode loyalty among opportunistic regime supporters dependent on state cash. The U.S. should be positioned for both, with a strategy to support protests and a framework for defections when they occur.

In September 2025, three months after the 12-Day War, Iran's late Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei warned of the state of 'neither war nor peace' that the enemy wants to impose on us, acknowledged sagging morale among the public, and lamented Iran's growing economic gap with other oil-rich nations. Four months later, millions took to the streets in an unprecedented uprising, the largest anti-regime movement in the Islamic Republic's history."

Tehran's Reshuffle Leaves No Pragmatic Opening
Saeed Ghasseminejad & Janatan Sayeh · FDD / Iran International · ~6 min

"Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei's latest appointments to Iran's top security and paramilitary institutions are not routine personnel changes but a reflection of his priorities for tighter control over the institutions directing war and domestic repression. The defining struggle in Tehran is not between moderates and hardliners. It is a contest within the hardline establishment and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) over power, patronage, and coercion.

The transition has occurred during a very turbulent time for the Islamic Republic, in the midst of a war with both the U.S. and Israel and only months after the largest anti-regime uprising. Mojtaba has also remained largely out of public view for months, fueling uncertainty over his status and capacity to lead. This vacuum gave rival security networks room to advance their own interests, but Washington should not mistake competition within the regime for a pragmatic opening."

"Water and energy infrastructure have become one of Iran's main levers of deterrence against the United States. Iranian Foreign Minister Aragchi recently made it clear to Gulf states that if the U.S. military renewed large-scale strikes on Iran, their critical infrastructure would be struck. Iranian forces' operational history over the past six months confirmed the threat's credibility. Kuwait first reported damage near a desalination plant early in Operation Epic Fury, when debris from an intercepted drone struck the Doha West facility. Then, on March 7 and 8, Iran claimed a desalination facility on Qeshm Island had been attacked."

The Right Argues With Itself
The Schittians [Sic] [more · less · never again]
Raw Egg Nationalist · In the Raw · ~9 min

"This is a direct follow-on from my piece about Rod Dreher, which began a series of exploratory essays about the conservative character. I'm trying to understand some of the more destructive quirks of conservatives and the movements they find themselves a part of, not because I want to poke fun at them, but because right-wingers need to do something about them if they actually want to win.

In four separate experiments, researchers from the University of Queensland, Australia showed nearly 5,000 adults in the US and UK a series of hypothetical scenarios designed to gauge their empathy for political allies and enemies. The researchers were shocked to discover that conservatives, not liberals, turned out to be the nice guys. 'Conservatives consistently showed more empathy to liberals than liberals showed to conservatives,' the researchers noted... In fact, I don't think these results are surprising at all. They're only surprising if you are yourself a liberal, and a naïve one at that, meaning you take yourself and other liberals at their own self-estimation: as the nicest, most moral people of all, the historical agents of equality, human rights, emancipation and Progress."

Aesthetics & the Good Life
Technological Determinism and Human Freedom [more · less · never again]
Evan Zimmerman · Palladium Magazine · ~15 min

"We fear that artificial intelligence will escape our control, pursue purposes of its own, displace human judgment, and ultimately reduce its creators to servants of the systems they built. These anxieties are often presented as unprecedented responses to an unprecedented technology. They are not. Long before computers, industrial machinery, or even the modern concept of 'technology,' human beings have told stories about inventions that rebelled against their makers, knowledge that brought catastrophe, and craftsmen destroyed by their own creations. Technological power has always appeared as both gift and danger. The waxen wings with which Icarus soars free from imprisonment fail him at his apex; the same Golem that safeguards the Jews of Prague almost proves the cause of their destruction.

This ambivalence is one of humanity's oldest and most consistent intuitions. Technology enlarges the range of what we can do, but in doing so changes the conditions under which we choose. It gives us power over nature while threatening to subject us to systems, incentives, and purposes that no individual intended.
