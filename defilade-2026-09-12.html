From the Field
The Three Obstacles [more · less · never again]
Bruce Ivar Gudmundsson, The Tactical Notebook, quoting Hilaire Belloc (1927) · ~6 min
"In the history of warfare obstacles to communication seem to fall into three categories. There are first those which are so formidable that military commanders 'take them for granted'... Such obstacles are the great mountain ranges, wide deserts, and (more rare) great stretches of marsh land. Obstacles of this first category are either wholly impossible or to be passed only at a few special points indicated by nature and improved by man. Thus, the Pyrenees have from the beginning of history been crossed, if I am not mistaken, by six passages and six alone.

The second category is that of obstacles which appear upon the map as formidable, but have proved in history to be hardly obstacles at all, because certain characters not at first apparent, rendered their franchise easy. Not a few great rivers fall into this category."
An Interwar Division [more · less · never again]
Bruce Ivar Gudmundsson, The Tactical Notebook · ~6 min
"In 1927, The Fighting Forces, a British journal dealing with military, naval, and aeronautical affairs, published a description of an infantry division of the California National Guard. Formed on a square framework inherited from the divisions of the American Expeditionary Forces, this formation consisted of two infantry brigades, a field artillery brigade, a medical regiment, a regiment of combat engineers, a light tank company, and a signal company... Both of the field artillery regiments rated by the field artillery brigade possessed a full peace-time allowance of ordnance, horses, and men. However, the brigade as a whole lacked both a headquarters and trains."

U.S. Space Command Is Testing Maneuver Warfare To Prepare for Next Generation of Fighting
Emmerson Overell, FDD · ~6 min
"It won't look like a dogfight from Star Wars or Battlestar Galactica, but in a future war, U.S. satellites may need to maneuver to survive. Space Command (SPACECOM) is practicing the right way to do it... Apollo Maneuvers drew inspiration from the U.S. Army's 1941 Louisiana Maneuvers, a series of exercises that tested ground tactics at scale before America entered World War II. Today, SPACECOM is similarly trying to determine what space warfare requires before commanders engage in combat above the atmosphere."

"Journalist Peter Bergen is back again with another book about terrorism, just in time for the 25th anniversary of the September 11, 2001 attacks.
