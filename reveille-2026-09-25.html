Ankara's Mediterranean ambitions

"The ongoing war with Iran is driving countries in the Middle East and the Eastern Mediterranean to invest in new security alliances... This partially helps explain why Turkey, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia signed the Mecca Pact in August. It is not, however, the driving factor behind why Greece, Israel and Cyprus are pushing to forge a closer security framework. Their main reason for investing in collective security is their growing fear of Turkish expansionism.

Turkey has lost no time capitalizing on Iran's weakening as a major regional actor, with President Recep Tayyip Erdogan constantly framing Turkey's desire to be respected as a 'global actor.' While the news cycle remains fixated on who is winning the Iran war, Ankara has quietly established footholds in Syria and across Africa through military basing, training and weapons sales; deepened strategic investments in Russia spanning energy and trade; adopted an increasingly revisionist posture across the Mediterranean, exemplified by its maximalist interpretation of its maritime borders under the 'Blue Homeland' doctrine; and built closer ties with major Arab powers intended to isolate and undermine Israel."

— Sinan Ciddi, FDD (via Kathimerini) · Read ›
