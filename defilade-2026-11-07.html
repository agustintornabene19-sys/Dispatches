the decade leading up to the outbreak of the First World War, the Ordnance Corps of the US Army developed a 'system of mobile artillery'. Six members of this seven-piece family were formed into duos, a gun and a howitzer that fired shells of the same sort. The remaining piece, a six-inch heavy field howitzer, stood alone. Bethlehem Steel manufactured three members of this family: the 3-inch light field gun, the 3.8-inch light field howitzer, and the 4.7-inch heavy field gun.
