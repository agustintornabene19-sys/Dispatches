Erdogan's "Pious Generation" Goes Global

"Late this past September, Turkey's Education Ministry entered into an agreement with the Ensar Foundation, an organization that claims to advance educational objectives in keeping with what it calls the teachings and requirements of Islam. This agreement authorized the foundation to organize a knowledge competition for high school students. A week before, the Diyanet Center of America, a significant religious institution near Washington, D.C., launched a leadership program for imams. And in June, a New York-based nonprofit established by two Erdogan-linked charities submitted its latest Foreign Agents Registration Act (FARA) report, detailing its activities on behalf of Turkish clients.

These developments are interconnected and form part of a wider, long-term initiative: President Recep Tayyip Erdogan's strategy to develop what he calls 'a pious generation.' Ongoing for more than a decade, this effort operates through Turkish government agencies and foundations affiliated with the Erdogan family, widening its reach from West Africa to suburban Maryland. In the United States, the Turken Foundation, which maintains family ties to the Erdogans, holds tax-exempt status as an educational charity. From the Branch

Looking Back at the Short Problems

"The sixteen 'short problems' published in the first issue of the Infantry School Mailing List gave students the opportunity to imagine infantry units of a new type, and, in particular, rifle squads that rated a double ration of Browning Automatic Rifles and regiments that carried more in the way of crew-served weapons than their predecessors... they often asked students to distinguish between courses of action that were suitable for well-trained, experienced troops and soldiers who were new to the nuances of active service. In addition, they also asked students to adjust their courses of action in the face of seemingly modest changes in circumstances.

At the same time, the component problems of this set lacked many of the details that were normally provided in decision games of the interwar period. In particular, the authors of the problems provided little in the way of context and, in particular, nearly nothing about the things that 'higher, adjacent, and supporting' units were doing."

— Bruce Ivar Gudmundsson, The Tactical Notebook · Read › [more · less · never again]
