Syria is off the terror list, but Washington still has leverage

"US President Donald Trump has fulfilled the promise he made to Syrian President Ahmad al-Sharaa in May 2025, to give Syrians 'a chance at greatness.' At the end of August of this year, Trump lifted the last major US designation and sanctions regime on Damascus, declaring that Syria is no longer a state sponsor of terrorism (SST). With the removal of the state sponsor of terrorism designation, Washington also delisted Hayat Tahrir al-Sham (HTS), the Islamist rebel group that Sharaa led before becoming Syria's president... The SST designation was not imposed on Syria's current leadership but on the former Assad regime in 1979, under Bashar al-Assad's father, Hafez al-Assad. It was later reinforced in response to Syria's backing of Hezbollah and other Iranian proxies. Until its removal, Syria had been among the longest-standing countries on the list, alongside Iran, Cuba, Venezuela, and North Korea."

— Ahmad Sharawi, FDD / The Algemeiner. Read ›

Machine Gun Man (IX)

"You command an army corps (XX Corps) of the Eighth Army of the German Empire. In addition to the usual elements of a German army corps of 1914 (two muscle-powered infantry divisions and one battalion of horse-drawn 15cm howitzers), you possess a motorized machine gun battalion. On 24 August 1914, elements of the recently arrived I Corps took over the position held by the motorized machine gun battalion north of Mlawa. After this relief, the motorized machine gun battalion occupied ground in the area north of Neidenburg and south of Allenstein, where it protected XX Corps from enemy forces coming from the east. Over the course of four days, the motorized machine gun battalion of XX Corps: delayed the advance of enemy formations, covered the deployment of friendly forces, covered the retreat of friendly forces, covered a gap between friendly formations."
