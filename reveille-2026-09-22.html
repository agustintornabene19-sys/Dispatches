"Mankeeping"
"'Mankeeping' is the central concept for a new study published in the journal Sex Roles. Researchers created a 46-point 'Mankeeping Scale' and administered it to hundreds of women in the US and the UK. The women were asked questions about 'the actual behaviors women perform to manage their partners' social life, the emotional impact of that labor, and a recognition that their emotional support is often unequal or unappreciated.'

The researchers found that women who saw themselves as doing more 'mankeeping' had 'lower relationship satisfaction and higher levels of relationship burden and burnout.' Doing extensive 'mankeeping' was also 'positively correlated with women's self-reported depression, anxiety and stress.' It wasn't simply recognizing that one's boyfriend or husband lacked friends and was struggling: it was having to do something about it that ended up having an 'emotional impact' and was 'associated with poorer mental health.' The researchers also found that women who 'held more traditional beliefs about gender roles' were more likely to engage in 'mankeeping,' and so to be less happy with the state of their relationship."
