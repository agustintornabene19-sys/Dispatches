FROM THE FIELD
Rebalancing Combined Arms: A Look at Future Force Structure [more · less · never again]
The Bazaar of War · ~6 min
"The modern system of combined arms discussed in last Dispatch shows no sign of going away in the 21st century. If anything, it is more important than ever before: the disciplined use of cover, concealment, suppression, and combined-arms integration have proven critical for the few offensive gains eked out on various battlefields in recent years.

In the second half of the twentieth century, for example, infantry, tanks, and artillery were often combined in tactical units at a 3:1:1 ratio. Depending on the time and place, those ratios could be preserved down to the battalion level, with a mortar battery serving as artillery. As drones have come into their own, this question of balance has come back to the fore. Drones in Ukraine at first mostly served to complement artillery by acting as spotters, then only as FPV drones proliferated principally become direct-strike weapons; in both cases, they have served the function of fires. Given the sheer number of drone units at all echelons, however, this has tilted the ratio of fires to infantry from the traditional 1:3 ratio toward something closer to parity."

AESTHETICS & THE GOOD LIFE
The Food Is Better in Europe [more · less · never again]
Raw Egg Nationalist, In the Raw · ~8 min
"It's become a commonplace of social-media health talk: "I went on holiday to Europe [from America] and all I did was eat croissants / gelato / pasta / bread etc. for a fortnight and still I lost 10 pounds! What's wrong with the food in America!?" There is something wrong with the food in America. The food is better in Europe. It's not just that Europe has deeper food traditions and the use of fresh local ingredients still remains the norm, but also that food is regulated in a different way. Americans do things with food and to food that can't be done in Europe. Take additives, for example. In the EU, there are around 2,000 additives licensed for use in food. In the US—nobody knows. The FDA's "Generally Recognised As Safe" ("GRAS") pathway has allowed food-makers to create new additives, declare that they're safe and introduce them to the food supply without even telling the FDA. This has been happening since 1958. Insane—there's no other word for it.

Even so, things aren't perfect over here. Far from it. Just like in America, agriculture and the food system in Europe are subject to power, money and the influence of corporate interests. That influence is only growing."
The seven sleepers, and good ole' Qitmir [more · less · never again]
The Pillar · ~8 min
"In the year 250, the Roman Emperor Decius decided to crack down on Christianity across his empire, and decreed that all Roman citizens had to offer a sacrifice of incense to the pantheon of Roman gods. In Ephesus, or maybe Jordan, or maybe a third unspecified location, there were seven rich young men who had grown up together, joined the military together, and who practiced the faith together. The young men tried to avoid the mandated sacrifice, but when they were eventually required to offer it by the military superiors, they refused.

They fled together to a cave in the mountains outside the city. The story says they prayed together in that cave, expecting eventually to be martyred. But it didn't go like that. Because one of them would slip periodically into the city to buy bread, their location was found out — but they weren't arrested. Instead, local officials went up to the cave, and sealed the entrance. The men were trapped inside to die. Until, as the story goes, God intervened. According to the story, the men were put into a deep sleep. They slept, it's said, for almost 200 years."
