The Long Reads

The midterm postmortem [more · less · never again]

Curtis Yarvin (Gray Mirror)

Republicans lost this election because they believe that elections are won by motivation, and motivation is about "issues" and "agendas." This is indeed how a republic works. The reality is that the United States is not a republic—a "res publica." It is not a government of informed, concerned citizens coming together to govern themselves better. It is a Third World democracy in which parties are armies. It is the late Roman Republic. In a Third World democracy, an election is a war: a contest for power. Ballots are bullets. Yes, wars need motivation. If history has any examples of war—foreign or civil, hot or cold—motivated by civic responsibility, I don't know them. No one ever fought a war for an "agenda." Wars are motivated by (a) loathing and (b) fear. But wars are fought by organization.

America’s grand strategy for a multipolar world [more · less · never again]

Engelsberg Ideas · Thomas Graham

A grand strategy of competitive co-existence proceeds from the premise that rivalry is a permanent feature of global affairs that can at best be managed. It accepts the limits on US power, especially to transform or dominate other major powers. It recognises that the destructive power of modern weaponry creates a moral and strategic imperative to avoid great-power conflict. And it acknowledges the necessity of leaving space for great-power cooperation to deal with critical transnational issues, such as climate change and nuclear proliferation, even as the great powers compete aggressively on other matters.
