Would honest speech about IQ end the world?

"As I may have mentioned once or twice across the decades, centuries, and millennia, a lot of bad decisions — such as Janet Napolitano banning applicants to the University of California from submitting their test scores or Nikole Hannah-Jones' whole career and family life — get made due to the social mandate to be ignorant about IQ science... Ever since Arthur Jensen's 1969 meta-analysis... the conventional wisdom among elites has been that scientists like Jensen, Richard Herrnstein and Charles Murray, and James D. Watson must be silenced to prevent the no doubt apocalyptic damage to society that would be sure to ensue if the facts were revealed."

"If these scientists were allowed to speak out without being shouted down, the public would soon turn into Nazis. Or something. Personally, I dunno what people would conclude. If the public learned more about the science, would they demand: 'Exterminate all the brutes!' Or would they feel: 'Half of humanity is below average in intelligence? Really? So you mean they're not just acting dumb to piss me off? Oh. Well, I guess we need to help them out …' Or would people think that it is potentially useful for them to become better informed on nature and nurture, which might have different implications for different policy issues? Why don't we have much of a clue what would happen? Well, one reason is because surprisingly little social science research has been performed on the topic."

You carry yourself wherever you go.

"Many years ago in Dublin, I had a Finnish colleague. Nina was her name... I asked her how she was finding Ireland — she'd only recently arrived. She said she'd spent years anticipating a move to Dublin: what it'd be like; the people she'd meet; the music events she'd go to; how she'd enjoy the culture; how she'd be and who she'd be. I asked her how it was now that she'd arrived. She said something like 'I brought myself with me. I didn't realise that would happen.'"

"This week, I was reading some sayings from the Desert Fathers and Mothers... One of her sayings is: 'We carry ourselves wherever we go, and we cannot escape temptation by mere flight.'... 'Temptation' is a strange word; I have old religious ghosts that rise up in me at the sound of it. What did Amma Matrona mean? The temptation of spending time wishing you were someone you're not? The temptation to destroy something that you feel excluded from? The temptation to reduce others to only one story?"
