Churchill, Maneuver, and the Stickiness of Language [more · less · never again]

The Bazaar of War

Maneuver theory is almost entirely the creation of William Lind, who, beginning in the 1970s, waged something of an insurgency within the American defense establishment to advocate doctrinal and force structure reforms. In the course of this, he wielded the maneuver-attrition paradigm as a polemical tool: to contrast the hidebound ways of the American military with the supposed virtues of past militaries (particularly the German). Placing attrition and maneuver at two ends of a spectrum was not only a category error , but created untenable definitions that bundled together too many unrelated concepts. "Maneuver" was not just maneuver, with its attendant prerogatives of speed, surprise, and initiative, but also came to encompass decentralized decision-making, psychological disorientation, and demoralization of the enemy. "Attrition" came to mean not just trading losses at a favorable rate, but static, deliberate, top-down-directed warfare supported by the lavish application of firepower.
