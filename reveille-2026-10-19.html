The Long Reads

Hegemonic Shocks and the Graveyards of World Order

"I define a hegemonic shock as a sudden shift in the distribution of relative power among the leading states in the international system. The term builds on the concept of 'hegemonic war' to include non-military shocks like global economic crises or imperial collapses—any period in which the power of one hegemon rises or declines significantly against the others. Three visible spikes of volatility: 1917–22, 1940–47, 1989–95... By producing clear winners and losers, shocks clarify the global distribution of power and allow opportunities for the creation of new international orders. In doing so, they become the graveyards and incubators of competing domestic regimes."

— Niccolo Soldo, Fisted by Foucault · Read › [more · less · never again]

March Separately, Strike Combined

"Between May 10 and 13, 1940, German Panzer Group Kleist advanced through the densely wooded Ardennes Forest, which the French considered impenetrable to large, mechanized formations. Numbering five armored and three motorized infantry divisions, totaling around 41,000 vehicles, the entire panzer group moved along just four Rollbahnen, or axes of advance, creating the largest traffic jam in military history. When the leading XIX Armored Corps, commanded by Gen. Heinz Guderian, converged on the French city of Sedan, it epitomized the concept of Schwerpunkt, or point of main effort. Following his maxim 'Klotzen, nicht kleckern' (hit with the fist, don't feel with...)."
