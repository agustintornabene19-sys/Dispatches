The Long Reads

Why America can't keep China down [more · less · never again]

UnHerd · Wolfgang Munchau

Far more significant than Huang herself was the generation of Chinese students laboring away on PhDs in Western science and engineering departments. In mathematics, I recall an instance when an entire year group of graduate students was recruited from China. That's unsurprising when British universities are critically reliant on Chinese students to remain solvent. Since we ourselves educated an entire generation of Chinese scientists and engineers, why are we surprised that we fail to keep them down, especially when China itself now boasts some of the top universities on Earth? In the end, you can't place an export ban on knowledge.

Le soleil noir de la gauche [more · less · never again]

Le Grand Continent · Louis Puel

Il faut accepter la réalité qui se présente à nous. Les tensions qui traversent le modèle économique et politique qui avait assuré la prospérité et la stabilité de la France et de l'Europe depuis trois quarts de siècle sont proches d'un point de rupture. La préservation de la justice, de la sécurité et de la soutenabilité est en train d'exercer sur l'État, en tant qu'acteur économique et stratégique, une pression telle qu'elle disloque le référentiel issu du passé. L'innovation technologique ne signifie plus automatiquement un haut niveau de croissance, le renouvellement de la population n'est plus acquis, la sécurité n'est plus garantie par des partenaires bienveillants, la stabilité écologique n'est plus un don gratuit de la nature. Tout ce qui formait autrefois les conditions pré-politiques de la légitimité démocratique, et que l'on pensait aller de soi, nous apparaît désormais comme un coût.

Are trans politics moderating? [more · less · never again]

UnHerd · Valerie Stivers

Feminist and queer theorists like Donna Haraway or Beatriz (now Paul) Preciado have long suggested radical, revolutionary, and political elements to the movement — but in the fine print, those elements were fundamentally incompatible with the power structure. To undermine gender norms was to undermine all hierarchical relations, creating a freer and more equal society, in which no one had too much power, theoretically. That isn't how things worked out when the movement first went mainstream and gathered power, however. Instead, radical gender politics proved all too genial to the suffocating managerial and HR politics of the 2010s and early 2020s, stoking a backlash in turn.
