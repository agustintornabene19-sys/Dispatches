The Houthis Are Hitting Saudi Arabia's Energy Pipeline — and Here's Why That Matters

"Saudi Arabia has weathered Iran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz by relying on its east-west pipeline to send crude from the kingdom's energy-rich eastern province to the Red Sea terminal of Yanbu. The port exports averaged more than 4 million barrels per day in June and early July — still a far cry from the more than 7 million bpd the kingdom exported in February, but better than other Gulf states with limited or nonexistent Hormuz alternatives.

But this pathway is vulnerable. The Houthis have their sights set on disrupting Saudi Arabia's alternative to the Persian Gulf to pressure their neighbor to the north. The Iran-backed terror group hopes escalation against Riyadh will earn them concessions that improve their position in Yemen while increasing Iran's leverage against the United States. The Houthis aren't planning to achieve this only through a maritime blockade. They are also targeting Saudi energy infrastructure."

From the Branch

Short Problem 11 — Infantry School Mailing List, 1931

"The scale of Problem Number 11 differs greatly from that of its predecessors. Where previous decision games in the series asked students to take on the roles of sergeants, lieutenants, and captains, this exercise puts participants in the role of a brigadier general who, true to his title, commands a reinforced brigade: a two-fisted collection composed of six battalions of infantry and six batteries of field artillery.

The clean correspondence between these basic building blocks... points to features frequently found in armies of the twentieth century. First, the reliable ratio of field batteries to infantry battalions made it possible to pair one of the former with one of the latter, thereby creating marriages that, if not entirely monogamous, promoted close cooperation. Second, the way that these building blocks fit together with others of the same type made it possible for larger units to create parallel pairings of their own... The chief complication of this arrangement lay in the realm of nomenclature. The US Army of 1930 used the term battalion to describe both the unit of account of infantry and a troika of field batteries."

— Bruce Ivar Gudmundsson, The Tactical Notebook · Read › [more · less · never again]
