Why the future is Henry the hoover [more · less · never again]

UnHerd · Aris Roussinos

On the other hand, the cheap and needlessly cheerful Henry, as any builder or renovator knows, will accept any amount of punishment with its trademark idiot smirk. Originally designed to clean out commercial boilers, the Henry is a second-order product of Britain's lost age of heavy industry, still assembled by hand in a Somerset factory. It does one job, sucking up dirt and dust, and does it as well as it needs to do, without communicating its performance, or user data, or its first flickering doubts and anxieties of consciousness, to distant data centers. When it comes to it, I don't need my hoover to decide what to clean, or how to do so most efficiently, or act as a high-performance showcase for innovation, but just to perform a simple task, well and reliably. It is more reliable, more robust, simply, because it is less complex. If only the British state were a Henry, we would all be in a much better place.

What it means to serve a country that doesn't know its military [more · less · never again]

Responsible Statecraft · Adam Weinstein

Our country is no longer accustomed to mass mobilization or staggering military casualties. A folded American flag is no longer a familiar feature of American living rooms. And whether Americans celebrated or condemned the deployment of National Guard troops to Washington and other cities, the reactions were unified in an underlying sense of novelty. Soldiers don't belong here. They belong over there. And when they return home, unless you live near a military base, they melt back into the general population. To much of the civilian population, military personnel are almost exotic figures, representatives of a far-away institution. The all-volunteer force frees the overwhelming majority of Americans from the obligation to serve. That freedom is an achievement, but it also creates a dangerous distance between those who decide when America goes to war, those who fight those wars, and the public in whose name they are fought.
