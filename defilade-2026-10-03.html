The Culture War

Who we are, Sophia's pitch, and made in China [more · less · never again]
The Pillar · ~6 min
One of the stories we have been tracking over the past few years is a movement in Europe, mostly centered in the Low Countries, in which Catholics have been suing the Church to erase their names from baptismal registers... the advocate general at the European Court of Justice issued an opinion stating that everyone has "in principle, the right to have his or her personal data erased from the baptismal register where the controller fails to demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds for the processing which override the interests, rights, and freedoms of the data subject."

I find the whole case fascinating. Now, on the one hand, I tend to skew in favor of telling institutions, usually companies and government agencies, to get my good name out of their filthy databases. But baptismal registers aren't mailing lists, they are historical records of fact. Indeed, a great deal of European history would be lost to us, at the purely factual level, were it not for the meticulous sacramental record-keeping of your average parish. It's somehow emblematic of our age that people want to erase their own identity — and to be clear, baptism at the most basic level imparts a character and forever changes our identity — while Europe as a culture is struggling to wipe clean its own history.

Revenge Is a Central Pillar of Iranian Foreign Policy
Reuel Marc Gerecht & Ray Takeyh, FDD / The Wall Street Journal · ~6 min
In Middle Eastern societies, seeking revenge is an acceptable way for men to cope with grief and wounded pride from the violent loss of loved ones. Since 1979 this approach has also been an important part of Iran's foreign policy. The export of the revolution, the assassination of antiregime expatriates, and terrorism against foreign enemies are means to get even for the injuries, real or imagined, the West has inflicted on Iran and Islam. There are many reasons Donald Trump's search for a diplomatic solution to the Iran war is unlikely to succeed, but high among them is that such a denouement wouldn't satisfy a vengeance-seeking Iranian elite. Even if Mr. Trump were to surrender — meaning Washington unfreezes Iranian assets, acknowledges Tehran's "right" to impose levies on ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz, and lifts the blockade on Iran's shipping and ports — it would likely encourage the Islamic Republic to seek more than the president's humiliation.
Russian Cyberespionage Campaign Signals Should Fast-Track Lessons Learned From Ukraine
Johanna Yang, FDD · ~7 min
Russia is testing its cyber weapons in Ukraine before turning them against the United States and its allies. Microsoft has disclosed that the Russian intelligence-linked hacking group Star Blizzard breached more than 100 organizations across the United States and the United Kingdom. But the research points to something more consequential than a single espionage campaign: Russian intelligence services are using the war in Ukraine as a proving ground for capabilities — and the United States has largely failed to recognize the value of this early-warning system.

Continental

Le gouvernement à l'école de la rue
Le Monde diplomatique · ~8 min · en français
En 1986, les cortèges étudiants et lycéens avaient contraint le gouvernement de Jacques Chirac à retirer le projet Devaquet. En 2006, les mobilisations de la jeunesse forçaient l'exécutif à abandonner le contrat première embauche. Quelle mesure répondrait aujourd'hui aux revendications lycéennes ? Recruter les professeurs manquants laisserait entière la question de la sélection. Supprimer Parcoursup ne fournirait pas les moyens d'accueillir les étudiants. Rénover les établissements n'abolirait pas l'angoisse d'un futur obscurci par l'IA et le climat. Le mouvement lycéen pointe au fond l'inconséquence générale des politiques menées depuis des décennies : gérer l'instant ; mépriser l'avenir.

Les gouvernements successifs ont installé l'adaptation individuelle comme réponse aux difficultés collectives. Les candidats perfectionnent leurs dossiers et les demandeurs d'emploi accumulent les expériences, tandis que les ressources familiales déterminent leur capacité à patienter ou à partir ailleurs. L'idéologie du mérite rapporte ensuite le résultat aux efforts de chacun... Au moment précis où se prépare un budget qui fera la part belle à la défense aux dépens des services publics, une jeunesse souvent décrite comme décérébrée par les réseaux sociaux construit une mobilisation pleinement politique.

Aesthetics & The Good Life

The Impossible City [more · less · never again]
Mary Harrington · ~10 min
Baudrillard said it's not possible for anywhere in Europe to be truly modern. Only America, he said, can do that. Having spent the last three nights in New York City, I'm minded to agree with him. New York is so photographed, filmed, and mythologised that there's surely nothing left to say. But honestly: it should be recognised as a wonder of the modern world, just as the Lighthouse at Alexandria was a wonder of the ancient one.

The other day, on a rooftop at UnHerd's just-opened event space in Midtown, chatting to someone I absently registered the vertical canyons of plate glass behind me like: "oh this must be painted on some huge canvas, nothing like that could possibly be real." But it's real. The same sense at 0530 this morning, running through Central Park. Now and then I'd glance up and there, in the gaps between trees, is this totally sci-fi backdrop, again just impossible and yet definitely there.

Morel's Invention, a new translation
Adolfo Bioy Casares, trans. Margaret Jull Costa — NYRB Classics · ~4 min
"A masterfully paced and intellectually daring plot. Like the best science fiction, of which this is an exemplar, Bioy's themes have become ever more relevant to a society beholden to image." —The Times (UK)

Jorge Luis Borges declared Morel's Invention a masterpiece, comparable to The Turn of the Screw and Journey to the Center of the Earth. When a fugitive washes up on a deserted island, he quickly discovers that the place is in fact populated by well-heeled tourists, whose nightly comings and goings are as bewildering as they are fascinating. Soon, the fugitive becomes enamored of a beautiful woman and finds himself more and more caught up in the island's mystery — until at last he realizes it is time for him to take action.
