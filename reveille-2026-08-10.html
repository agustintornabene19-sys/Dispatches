What we saw in Wisconsin

Irie Sentner, POLITICO Playbook

"Francesca Hong has some regrets. After spending a week under intense scrutiny for a series of old, since-deleted tweets about topics from abolishing the police to her disapproval of Thanksgiving, Hong admitted as much during a campaign stop here. 'I had some hot takes, some bad takes on Twitter, but when you're a business owner running a restaurant during Covid, trying to pay bills as a student, you're putting feelings out there into the world,' Hong said... 'But I've evolved, I've learned. I think it's more dishonest not to learn.'

That Hong is still wrestling with — and moderating on — posts from years ago is emblematic of Wisconsin's gubernatorial Democratic primary. It's here that the progressive movement's midterms march has arrived, as the democratic socialist Hong is forcing establishment Democrats to mount an all-out, last-ditch effort to halt her momentum... The verdict: Electability is top of mind for voters and both candidates, as each races to prove they can build the widest, strongest coalition against presumptive Republican nominee Tom Tiffany."

From the Branch

Solutions to Short Problem 12

Bruce Ivar Gudmundsson, The Tactical Notebook

"The northern regiment would attack with two battalions in assault and one (the one which was somewhat disorganized) in reserve. The southern regiment would attack in column of battalions, the last one being in brigade reserve... The attack should be an envelopment of the southern flank (of the enemy force). There is better cover there, and ridges are parallel to direction of attack. It is desirable for the sake of simplicity for one regiment to make the secondary and the other make the enveloping attack, and not have to attack any of the front of the enemy position.

Commentary: Problem 12 requires students to draw unit boundaries before asking them to devise a scheme of maneuver. In other words, it puts the cart before the horse. The solution compounds this error by presuming a plan for an envelopment from the south. With these things in mind, I find myself wondering... That a clerical error removed the scheme of maneuver from the description of the problem. That the author of the problem made deliberate mistakes in order to provoke pushback from his students."
