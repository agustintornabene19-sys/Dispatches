The Moral Crisis of Social Security [more · less · never again]

Law & Liberty · Thomas Savidge

In the 236 years since Burke penned those words, that partnership increasingly resembles a conflict over the obligations inherited from the past and passed onto the future. Nowhere is this more apparent than in Social Security's Old Age and Survivors Insurance (OASI). Retirees have organized their lives around promised benefits while younger workers finance those benefits and face uncertainty about their own future. As demographic and financial pressures intensify, the program creates competing claims between generations. Social Security's designers bear responsibility for creating a system that rewards demands for benefits while passing substantial costs to younger and future taxpayers. Politicians gain support by promising payments while organized beneficiaries defend transfers financed by others. Successive generations have inherited both the bill and the incentives to enlarge it.
