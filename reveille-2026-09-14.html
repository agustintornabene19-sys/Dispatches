A.I.: "Solution Unsatisfactory?"

"The rapid emergence of AI is reminiscent in some ways (but not in others) of the sudden emergence of nuclear technology from 1938 onward. Note that we eventually worked out a strategy of mutual assured destruction that, so far at least, has provided some stability. But it took a couple of decades to fully figure it out. One of the first attempts to think through the long run institutional implications of radioactive weapons was made by science fiction author Robert A. Heinlein in December 1940, after discussions with his editor John W. Campbell and his friend, physicist Robert A. Cornog of Ernest O. Lawrence's radiation laboratory at Berkeley... The long short story that resulted, 'Solution Unsatisfactory,' should not be seen as a prediction of any one outcome or endorsement of any one policy. But it remains an impressive early exercise in trying to work out the logical implications of a new world-historical technology."

From the Branch

Specialists or Soldiers?

"On 23 May 1911, Gerald Francis Ellison appeared before the Committee on the Training and Organization of the Royal Engineers... 'My experience as Director of Organization has led me to the conclusion that there are two distinct types of men required in the Army, each of which must be treated on wholly different lines. The types are, first, the fighting soldier, and, secondly, the highly skilled artisan and tradesman. The more we attempt to mix up these two types, or treat them with rigid uniformity, the greater are the consequent confusion, waste of money, and loss of efficiency.'"

"Taking the tailoring trade alone... Of the boys enlisted, eighty-one per cent have practically no previous training in tailoring work. The training that they receive in the regimental tailors' shops has proved to be quite elementary in character. As a result, the so-called tailors, who are the product of boy enlistment, are little more than mere stitchers... The actual result of the present system is that only sixteen indifferent tailors are produced for every hundred boys enlisted."

— Bruce Ivar Gudmundsson, The Tactical Notebook  |  Read › [more · less · never again]
