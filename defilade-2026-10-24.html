Aesthetics & the Good Life
Kindling the Fire from Heaven [more · less · never again]
Raw Egg Nationalist · In the Raw · ~14 min
"In my journeys through various archives, I discovered another series of printed sermons that suggested the argument of a very famous book, David Underdown's Fire from Heaven, was seriously overstated. Underdown argued that the Dorset county town of Dorchester was transformed into the most Puritan town in England after a great fire in 1613, which the local preacher, John White (one of the founders of Massachusetts), argued was a judgment from God on the sins of the townsfolk. With the evidence I found, it became clear that there was already a vigorous attempt to reform the character of the town decades before the Great Fire. Underdown had totally missed this."

"How quickly Protestantism took root in Dorchester we can only guess. In the sixteenth century the town had no great reputation as a centre of reformation; it was Poole that was said to have contained 'the first in that part of England were called Protestants.' Central to the story of David Underdown's book, Fire from Heaven, is his claim that between the accession of Elizabeth I, in 1558, and the Great Fire of 1613, which destroyed most of the town, Dorchester was essentially an unregenerate place."
