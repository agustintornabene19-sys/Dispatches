The Bernie-dogs: fusing Sanders and the Blue Dogs — POLITICO Playbook

"Bob Brooks in Pennsylvania, Rebecca Cooke in Wisconsin, Sam Forstag in Montana and Brian Poindexter in Ohio have all pulled off an unlikely pair of endorsements: Sen. Bernie Sanders (I-Vt.) and the Blue Dogs' leadership PAC. These might seem like strange bedfellows, occupying the most progressive and most moderate flanks of elected Democrats. But the Bernie-dogs appealed to both not by splitting the difference and landing in an establishment space. Instead, they say they're scrambling the ideological binary and leaning into a working-class populism.

When Cooke met with Sanders in Washington, he started by asking: "You're not a progressive. Why do you want my endorsement?" "I can't think of somebody that is more representative of the working class and has been more of a champion of the working class," she said she told him.

All four candidates have working-class backgrounds or jobs — as a firefighter, server, smokejumper and ironworker. And all four are running to flip districts Donald Trump won in 2024, where the imperative for Democrats to convince voters who've soured on the party is urgent.

"[Sanders is] making a judgment that these working-class candidates stand a really good chance of overperforming in these elections," said Faiz Shakir, one of the senator's senior advisers who ran Sanders' 2020 campaign. "He's had a concern, long concern, with the Democratic Party brand that it is not affiliated with working-class voters enough."

From the Branch

Minimum Viable Force: Operational Considerations for Future Force Design — The Bazaar of War

"What is the smallest-sized ground force that can operate in a single theater against a first-rate peer competitor? That is to say, what is the minimum force size to remain operationally viable? The answer depends first and foremost on the strength of the enemy, and secondarily on geography. But beyond those considerations, there are more fundamental variables involved. A force with true operational capability must be able to perform a variety of tasks on demand: move, dig in, sustain itself, conduct reconnaissance, cross obstacles, protect its lines of communication, etc. Each of these tasks requires specialized units to perform, and may also require combat troops and assets for security, detracting from overall combat power. Below a certain threshold, all of a unit's strength must go to merely sustaining itself, not to performing its mission.

We see perhaps the earliest example of a formal minimum viable force in Napoleon's corps d'armée. These were the building blocks of his armies, with enough operational independence to march tens of kilometers apart from their neighbors for weeks at a time if necessary—useful for the ambitious, sweeping maneuvers he was fond of. As the smallest unit that organically combined infantry, cavalry, and artillery, a corps was famously able to hold out against any enemy it was likely to encounter long enough for neighboring corps to come to their aid. Tactical endurance was a necessary condition for this limited operational independence, but not sufficient. Any unit could combine infantry, cavalry, and artillery in well-balanced proportions in line of battle; what was truly the governing factor at the operational level was instead the absolute quantity of cavalry."
