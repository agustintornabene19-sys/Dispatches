From the Branch

Tanks in Winter

"If they are to remain fit to fight, men on active service in wintertime need opportunities to warm themselves, prepare hot drinks, and make hot soup. For that reason, built-up areas play a bigger role in winter than they do in other seasons... Units dependent upon built-up areas find it harder to form continuous fronts. As a result, units operating in wintertime enjoy more frequent opportunities for breakthroughs, infiltrations, and envelopments than units serving in other seasons. Units of the armored troops must practice the rapid seizure of built-up areas, using techniques that make allowance for inclement weather and the limited hours of daylight... Winter weather damages roads, thereby making it more difficult to provide tanks with fuel and other supplies. As a result, leaders must make careful preparations for the movement and storage of such items."

— Bruce Ivar Gudmundsson, The Tactical Notebook · Read › [more · less · never again]
