The Right Argues With Itself

The Floundering Anglo-American Right [more · less · never again]

Andrew Sullivan, The Weekly Dish · ~8 min

"Two big speeches this week from two possible future conservative leaders: the British Tory leader, Kemi Badenoch, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio. She played it safe; he reached for a new synthesis. And both, it seems to me, failed. There was plenty in Badenoch's annual address to her party conference to warm an old-school Thatcherite heart. A classic: We believe in the dignity of work, they believe it's fine to live a life on benefits. We believe prosperity comes from people and businesses, they believe it comes from Government. But another classic missed its moment: I don't want to explain to my son when we're watching our brilliant England team play football that some people don't think our black players are English. ... Stirring, admirable stuff. For 2010. But for 2026, when one in five Brits, and two out of five Londoners, were not even born in the UK? When the country's heartland is experiencing an unprecedented, sudden break with a thousand years of demographic stability? In just a few decades, the indigenous population of the UK will become a minority on its own island — in large part because of Tory policies."

A sharper diagnosis of Badenoch's conference speech appears, under the headline "Who is Kemi Badenoch kidding?", in UnHerd — arguing simply that "demonizing Reform makes victory impossible."

Vatican Confidential, a New Way of Being Synod, and Merch Wars [more · less · never again]

The Pillar · ~7 min

"The big story this week in the Church is the meeting currently happening in Rome, convened by Pope Leo to mark the tenth anniversary of Amoris Laetitia... I was struck by a particular plea of the pope's in his opening remarks on Wednesday. Leo asked 'that we all preserve the discretion and confidentiality that this time of listening and discernment requires.' Don't talk to the press, in other words... And to a great degree I sympathize with the pope(s). If you want people's honest opinions on complicated issues, opinions which often require nuanced drawing out, it's hard to get them if everyone is worried about being (or eager to be) soundbited. But the thing about these meetings, with their round tables and break out sessions and small groups, is that what is discussed is highly cabined... and a lot of power to shape the narrative, so to speak, is necessarily invested in organizers and those charged with 'summing up' what was said across however many groups."

Britain & Europe

Time for Europe To Stop Funding Islamist Networks

Simone Rodan-Benzaquen, FDD · ~6 min

"Europe's Parliament wants to close the public purse to Muslim Brotherhood networks. Its recent vote challenges a funding system that risks subsidizing organizations whose political agenda undermines the democratic freedoms Europe seeks to defend. Lawmakers voted 381 to 167 on October 8 in favor of a strategy countering Islamist efforts to gain influence inside European institutions... Founded in Egypt in 1928, the Brotherhood seeks to organize society, including government, around its interpretation of Islam. Its European infrastructure spans religious associations, charities, and youth organizations. A 2025 French government report identified the Council of European Muslims, founded in 1989 and based in Brussels, as the movement's European umbrella organization, with a UK-registered charity, Europe Trust, serving as its financial arm and holding some 20 million pounds in property. In France alone, the report linked 139 places of worship, 21 private schools, and some 280 associations to the Brotherhood."

Continental

Espagne, la grève contre la rente

Le Monde diplomatique · ~8 min

"Une expulsion empêchée, un immeuble occupé, une assemblée de quartier… En Espagne, la lutte pour le logement a depuis longtemps dépassé la défense des ménages menacés d'expulsion. Elle a formé des militants, inventé des pratiques de solidarité et contribué à transformer la vie politique du pays. Quinze ans après les « indignados », les mobilisations actuelles prolongent cette histoire, alors que la crise du logement ébranle de nouveau les équilibres politiques. L'expulsion de Maricarmen Abascal, 87 ans, le 23 septembre à Madrid, puis sa mort le 7 octobre, ont nourri une mobilisation qui s'étend à plusieurs villes du pays... Le 11 novembre, une grève générale de vingt-quatre heures aura lieu à l'initiative, notamment, du « Sindicato de Inquilinas ». Elle bénéficie du soutien conjoint des deux principales centrales ouvrières, l'Union générale des travailleurs (UGT) et les Commissions ouvrières (CCOO), une première depuis quatorze ans."

Hezbollah After Nasrallah

Hezbollah's New Leader Naim Qassem Is No Hassan Nasrallah

David Daoud, FDD / The National Interest · ~6 min

"This past week, US officials were busy talking about disarming Hezbollah, pressing the visiting Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam to disrupt Hezbollah's finances and move toward disarming the group. Hezbollah, meanwhile, focused on housing. On September 27, which marked the second anniversary of Hassan Nasrallah's assassination, Hezbollah Secretary-General Naim Qassem defended the group's arsenal at length but dwelled on more mundane material concerns, especially reconstruction and housing for the displaced... Qassem's focus may seem badly mismatched to the scale of Hezbollah's predicament as Israeli military and diplomatic pressure mounts."

Hezbollah's Financial Lifeline Extends Beyond Iran's Cash Transfers

David Daoud & Ahmad Sharawi, FDD · ~6 min

"U.S. sanctions have strained Iran's finances and disrupted some funding channels to its Lebanese proxy, Hezbollah. Yet sanctions have not eliminated the group's access to outside funding, including support from organizations outside Hezbollah's formal hierarchy. On October 7, two sources told Reuters that Hezbollah received $200 million from Iran in September for Lebanese displaced by the war. The sources claimed that unspecified intermediaries 'charged 20 percent' on these transfers but did not explain how the money reached Lebanon... Treasury reported that Iran sent Hezbollah more than $1 billion through money exchanges in 2025. Hezbollah also benefits from organizations outside its formal hierarchy that finance relief and reconstruction in coordination with it, including WaTaawanou, a Lebanese charity relying heavily on crowdfunding."
