From the Branch

Leading Anti-Tank Units

"The majority of after-action reports from anti-tank (Panzerjäger) units mention the following issues: the subordination of anti-tank units to other branches of service, and the employment of self-propelled anti-tank guns... Reports invariably complain that placing anti-tank companies and platoons under the command of officers of other arms deprives anti-tank commanders of any influence over the deployment and use of their anti-tank weapons; that infantry units select defensive positions where anti-tank guns cannot provide them with adequate, or indeed any, protection against tanks; that officers of other arms order anti-tank units to open fire at ranges that exceed the effective reach of their weapons; and that officers of other arms deploy heavy anti-tank guns, whether towed or self-propelled, on open, exposed ground.

Only those who show an understanding for the concerns of others can reasonably expect others to show an understanding for their own requirements. An anti-tank gunner who, when employing his anti-tank weapons, considers not only how best to destroy enemy tanks, but also how to protect the combined-arms force, thereby prevents friction from developing in the first place. An anti-tank gunner who proactively seeks to position his anti-tank guns well forward will rarely encounter unreasonable requests."

— Bruce Ivar Gudmundsson, The Tactical Notebook · Read › [more · less · never again]
