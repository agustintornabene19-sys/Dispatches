America's Must-Win Battle of Hormuz

"To judge a war as it unfolds is risky. Winners and losers can change rapidly. Nonetheless, the continuing scrimmages in the Persian Gulf, the fluctuation in oil prices, the intensifying fear among the Arab Gulf states of a longer, larger conflict, and Donald Trump’s erratic and sometimes bizarre commentary have long suggested American confusion and Iranian advantage. Now add the possibly game-changing recent advance of Iran-allied Houthis along the Red Sea, the successful drone attack on Saudi Arabia’s East-West oil pipeline by Iran-allied Iraqi militias, and Mr. Trump’s hesitancy to respond to both. Without more effective American military action or an Iranian economic collapse, a U.S. victory seems elusive."

Cohn of Shame

"'From beyond the grave,' writes Fintan O'Toole in the magazine's October 8 issue, Roy Cohn 'has forced himself into American history.' Cohn has been dead for forty years, but, as a new biography demonstrates, his venal, mendacious, obscene, amoral approach to life and politics haunts the country to this day: At once not quite real and all too real, Cohn has the uncanny presence of a ghoul or a fiend. He is a creature from the depths of the American political imagination, too pure in his utter malignity to be entirely credible, too potent in his continuing consequence to be safely interred in the past. Cohn made his reputation helping send Julius and Ethel Rosenberg to the electric chair — in typically corrupt fashion, 'discussing the evidence behind the scenes with Irving Kaufman, the judge, and urging him to impose the death sentence' — and helping Joseph McCarthy persecute gay men and alleged Communists."

— Fintan O'Toole, The New York Review of Books · Read ›

From the Branch

10 Ways the U.S. Can Strengthen Deterrence in the Taiwan Strait

"Taiwan is not merely a democratic partner in a strategically vital region, it is the single most important technology supplier the American economy depends upon, a frontline of the global contest between democratic governance and authoritarian control, and the model ally the United States has spent a decade demanding its other partners become... Trading Taiwan's security away for trade concessions or diplomatic atmospherics with Beijing would not be dealmaking. It would be a strategic blunder of historic proportions, one that every ally from Tokyo to Warsaw would remember for a generation."

Among the ten: accelerate FMS deliveries and propose $10–15 billion in annual sales; bolster TERA-mandated bilateral naval and air exercises; and establish a persistent rotational U.S. Army Combat Arms Training Team in Taiwan.

— RADM (Ret.) Mark Montgomery, FDD · Read ›
