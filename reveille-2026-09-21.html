Decisive Battle and Exploitation: the irreducible whole of decision

"Anyone with even a passing interest in military history can point to Case Yellow, Germany's 1940 invasion of France, as an example of decisive victory... What is far less commonly known is that the hardest fighting was yet to come. The Germans suffered the majority of their casualties in Case Red, the three weeks between Dunkirk and the surrender, during which they fought through a mesh of fortified positions south of the Somme."

"But suppose, contrary to all expectation, the Germans dawdled north of the Somme? How much harder would their ultimate victory be? Every week's delay would give the French more time to mobilize, reorganize, and fortify. Factories around Paris continued to churn out tanks, allowing them to slowly reconstitute their shattered armored formations... The Allies' air forces suffered from deeper problems—they had sustained very high loss rates in desperate counterattacks around Sedan—but the majority of France's aircraft production was far from the front."
