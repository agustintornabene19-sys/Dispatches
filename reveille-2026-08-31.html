How the King must be played

"The British Monarchy is entering a vulnerable phase akin to the early 1990s. Ipsos polling from the start of the year shows that only 37% of British people now think we would be worse off if we abolished the monarchy, numbers we last saw in the mid 1990s (in 1992 it fell to 37%). By comparison in 2012 in the lead-up to the Diamond Jubilee this figure was 67%.

This fall has been driven as it was in the 1990s by exposure of the Royal family's venal degeneracy. In the 2020s this has been caused by Andrew's hanky panky and his mother's decision to cover for him. In the early 1990s it was Royal philandering. 1992, the annus horribilis, was the year that Diana published a book discussing Charles's affair with Camilla Parker-Bowles... During the 1990s it was received wisdom amongst most columnists and politicians that the Monarchy would not survive the death of the Queen — that the institution would die with her."

The mail ballot mess

"The country's first mail ballots for November are due to be sent out this week — but the method used by one-third of voters has a cloud of uncertainty hanging over it. The prospect of new U.S. Postal Service rules to tighten up mail voting and demand voter lists from states, given new juice by a Supreme Court decision last week, could inject some last-minute logistical chaos into election administration.

For President Donald Trump, the USPS regulations would achieve the crackdown on mail voting he's long sought in an effort to stamp out election fraud, despite a lack of evidence that such fraud exists on a broad scale. The Postal Service would refuse to deliver ballots for states that don't comply. And the Justice Department has dismissed worries about the changes as 'doomsday assumptions.'

'It's another blatant and obvious attempt by the Trump administration to pick their own voters,' Arizona Secretary of State Adrian Fontes told Playbook. 'This is a piss-poor policy, and it's being recklessly shoved down the throats of the entire nation because of one person's grievance.'"
