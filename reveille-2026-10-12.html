Bernhardi Scores a Bull's Eye
Bruce Ivar Gudmundsson, The Tactical Notebook
"In The War of the Future, Friedrich von Bernhardi recommended that the army of the postwar German Republic adopt an infantry battalion composed of three rifle companies, a machine gun company, and a 'worker' company (Arbeitskompagnie)... Bernhardi's scheme fell afoul of the 'army of leaders' of Hans von Seeckt. After all, in a force composed entirely of future field marshals, there's no need to create holding pens for lads that are hard-o'-hearing, near-o'-seeing, or far-too-fond-of-sneezing for Stoßtrupp duty.

Bernhardi did better with his design for the artillery of the new German Army. In keeping with his recommendations, the Reichswehr eliminated the distinction between the techie Foot Artillery (Fußartillerie) and horsy Field Artillery (Feldartillerie), thereby merging the two branches into a single arm. Bernhardi also scored a bull's eye with his proposal to provide each German infantry division with a heavy artillery battalion. Armed with eight 15cm heavy field howitzers and four 10cm heavy field guns, this unit remained on the drawing board until 1935. However, once Germany renounced the Treaty of Versailles, just about every German division, whether infantry, mountain, motorized, or armored, got one of these."
