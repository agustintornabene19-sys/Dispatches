Turkey's Justification for Military Presence in Syria Doesn't Hold Up

"'We are maintaining our military presence in Iraq and Syria,' Turkish Defense Minister Yasar Guler declared on October 20. Reprising comments he made in February, when he asserted that Turkey 'doesn't care what others say,' Guler said Ankara would reconsider its military presence only once its regional and border security conditions were met, without specifying what that would entail. Turkey maintains a substantial military presence in Syria, with approximately 20,000 troops stationed across multiple bases and positions in the country's north. Historically, Ankara has justified its presence by citing the threat posed by the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF)... However, with the SDF integrating into the Syrian army and Damascus rejecting the establishment of an autonomous Kurdish entity along the Turkish border, Ankara's justification for maintaining its military presence is increasingly untenable."

— Ahmad Sharawi, Foundation for Defense of Democracies · Read ›
