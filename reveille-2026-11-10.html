The Long Reads

Republicans Are Losing the Populist Moment [more · less · never again]

Compact · Alicia Nieves

In just twenty-two months, that opportunity evaporated. The Republican Party's sweeping defeat in the 2026 elections was not simply another instance of voters using the midterms, as they so often do, to punish the president's party for missteps during his first two years in office. Nor is it adequate to explain the results solely through voter frustration over higher prices, the Iran war, or corruption. Those issues undoubtedly fractured Trump's electoral coalition and damaged both his personal approval rating and the GOP's broader standing with voters. But underlying these political liabilities was a more fundamental failure. Trump and the Republican establishment never forged the workable political relationship needed to transform the raucous populist insurgency that swept them into power into a coherent, pragmatic governing agenda.

The lesson of l’affaire Huang [more · less · never again]

UnHerd · Mary Harrington

Sex, power, and geopolitics are never far apart. In 1917 the Dutch exotic dancer Margaretha Zelle, better known by her stage name "Mata Hari", was executed by firing squad, accused of espionage. To the end, she denied the charge, and later historians have claimed she was scapegoated. But however unfairly, "Mata Hari" entered popular usage, as a byword for that most potent of femmes fatales : the seductive, treacherous "honeypot". Now, as the AI race hots up, Mata Hari has come for the tech world. Observers on either side of the US-China rivalry might wonder: are the tech world's nerdy denizens ready for a frontline role in the grandest geopolitical struggle of our time?

After Good Faith: Intelligence Oversight in the New Congress [more · less · never again]

Lawfare · Julia Curlee

How could this happen? Iran was not an intelligence failure in which analysts missed a threat. It was a policy failure in which experts warned decision-makers who acted against it anyway. The White House, at war with a mythical "deep state," had dismantled the National Security Council (NSC), the staff that ensures warnings reach the president before he decides, and publicly fired intelligence officers who would not tell him what he wanted to hear. A president who guts the decision-making process and purges his experts will keep blundering into foreseeable—and foreseen—debacles.
