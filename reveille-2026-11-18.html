The American Conservative · Jennifer Kavanagh

Any discussion over how to end the war must address the shape of Europe's security order for the simple reason that it is the flaws, shortcomings, and inconsistencies of the existing system that led to the outbreak of the conflict in the first place. Washington should not, however, underestimate the challenges involved in updating Europe's security arrangements. A lasting solution will require not just European buy-in, but European leadership and the development of rules capable of managing what will be a contested and adversarial relationship between Russia and its neighbors for many years to come.

L’hallucination douguinienne de la tripolarité [more · less · never again]

Le Grand Continent · Louis Puel

Conçus pour contester l'hégémonie états-unienne, les modèles géopolitiques de Schmitt et Haushofer s'inspirent pourtant largement du schéma impérial fourni par les États-Unis. Tous deux se réclament en effet de la doctrine Monroe (énoncée en 1823 par James Monroe et renforcée en 1904 par Théodore Roosevelt), soit de l'idée qu'une puissance disposerait d'une aire d'influence exclusive bien plus vaste que son seul territoire national, en l'occurrence l'ensemble du continent américain pour les États-Unis. Haushofer et Schmitt souhaitent dupliquer la doctrine Monroe à d'autres régions du monde, qui seraient chacune appelées à être dominées de manière exclusive par une grande puissance.

The genius of Theodore Roosevelt’s New Nationalism [more · less · never again]

Engelsberg Ideas · David Cowan

Roosevelt's memory has often been co-opted by the Left, but he deserves to have his place in the Right's pantheon, too. The reason is that he understood how capitalism could only endure if prosperity could be enjoyed by as many people as possible. Roosevelt represented the politics of broad-based prosperity. In Roosevelt's time, rapid industrialisation had transformed the United States, but also brought a host of major new problems. Poverty, exploitation, poor public health, low levels of education, unemployment, insecure work and so on, afflicted the lower orders of society. The American middle class was growing, but not fast enough.
